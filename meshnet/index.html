Universal check-in net · MeshCore

#meshnet

One channel, two evenings a week, ten minutes. Everyone on MeshCore checks in at the same moment, wherever they are, so the whole mesh is tested at once. No net control and no sign-up. Add the channel. Check in. Answer what you hear. This page is the home of #meshnet for Indiana, with Indiana's check-ins on the map below. Any other mesh group can mirror it for its own area.

    How to check in

    1. In the MeshCore app, add the hashtag channel #meshnet, all lowercase. New to MeshCore? Start here.
      Add #meshnet in the app

      On your phone, tap the button; on a computer, scan the code with the MeshCore app. Needs app version 1.47 or newer. The link uses MeshCore's channel QR format.

    2. Between 7:00 and 7:10 PM US Eastern, post your check-in. Send it at least five times over the ten minutes; the repeats are what load the mesh, and only one is logged. Testing more than one radio? Check in from each, at least five times on each; every radio is logged. Tip: type it before 7:00 and copy it, so each send is just a paste.

      KUMP, Heltec V3, 4 months

      Start with your closest small airfield code, KXXX. The device and months are welcome in any order, and commas help but aren't required: KUMP, KUMP Heltec V3, and KUMP 4 months Heltec V3 all count.

    3. Acknowledge each check-in you hear with a mention and a greeting, or just "heard".

    Chat on #meshnet before, during, and after the net is welcome. Only 6:57 to 7:13 PM is logged, and those minutes are the time to load the mesh.

    Help it grow. Invite others the usual ways: tell a neighbor, mention it in your local group's chat or Discord, announce it on your local ham or GMRS net, or share the invite. Every new check-in shows how far the mesh reaches.

    If there is severe weather or any emergency on the mesh, the net is postponed. Emergency traffic always comes first.

    Indiana's check-ins

    Loading the check-in log…

    Logged automatically on Sundays and Thursdays only, from 6:57 to 7:13 PM US Eastern (the net plus three minutes each side); nothing outside that window is recorded. Each net shows up here after it ends, usually within a few hours; during the net, the channel itself is the live view. The log reads the #meshnet messages community observers upload to OKI Mesh's CoreScope and Chicagoland Mesh's CoreScope, one check-in per radio. No names or callsigns are kept. A check-in shows here only if an observer heard it, so more observers means a fuller map. Meshtastic check-ins are not logged.

    Why check in
    • Test your radio and your settings.
    • See who your messages reach, and who reaches you.
    • Practice, so it's routine when the mesh is needed.
    • Meet the people on the mesh near you, and in the next region over.

Why it matters
A mesh that works on a quiet night can fall apart when everyone needs it at once. Posts spread across the day rarely collide, so they tell us little. Ten tight minutes with everyone posting together loads repeaters the way a real emergency would and shows where messages get through. One shared time across every group also tests the links between neighboring meshes.

    When something fails

    Finding failures is the point of the net. Each one tells you what the mesh needs next.

    Nobody heard you
    Your area needs a repeater with height and a good antenna. Put one up (the MeshCore card in Settings has a Repeater tab), or offer a high site to someone who will.
    You got out, but you're not on the map
    No observer heard you. Add one that feeds a public network.
    Your observer feeds a different CoreScope
    Let us know on GitHub or the Indiana Mesh Discord, and it can be added to the log.

    Mirror it in your area

    #meshnet works because it is the same everywhere. No one owns it. Any mesh group, club, collective, or organization is welcome to run it, announce it under its own name, and make it its own.

    Keep the same

    • The channel: #meshnet
    • The days: Sundays and Thursdays
    • The time: 7:00 to 7:10 PM US Eastern, for every region
    • The check-in: airport code first, then device and months
    • Postponed for severe weather or any emergency on the mesh

    The shared time is the whole point. New users find a busy channel to test against, and advanced users see the whole mesh, including the links between neighboring meshes, under load at once.
