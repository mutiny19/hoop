Get on the Indiana mesh

From a new radio to your first message in five steps. You'll hear everything the repeaters around you pass along. Regions are rarely used; ask people in your area before setting one.

Default: openHear everythingRegions: ask locally
On regions: ask before you set one. Region usage is rare. Most traffic in Indiana goes out unscoped, which reaches the most contacts, including across state lines. Check with others in your area whether they use regions on their repeaters or companions, and match what they do. If a channel gets too busy, muting or leaving it is simpler than scoping it. Repeater owners: see the official MeshCore docs.

First radio to first message

Get a radio

Two safe first picks, roughly $20 to $40, both on the MeshCore flasher list: the SenseCAP T1000-E, a credit-card-sized tracker with its own battery and antenna, nothing to assemble; or a Heltec V3 or V4 board, cheaper and more flexible, which you'll want a case and a small battery for. Buy the US 915 MHz version of either. Any other device on the flasher list works too.

Not sure? Ask in the Indiana Mesh Discord before you order; someone will tell you what they'd buy today. The MeshCore FAQ has more on what you need to start.

Install Companion firmware

What's "Companion"? It means a radio you carry and control from your phone. Rooftop relay boxes run the other kind, Repeater; you don't need one to start.

Open the MeshCore flasher in Chrome or Edge on a computer, plug the radio in by USB, pick your device and choose the Companion (Bluetooth) firmware.

Install the app and pair

Get the MeshCore app for iPhone, Android or the web. Connect to your radio over Bluetooth and give yourself a name.

Set the US radio settings

In the radio settings pick the USA preset. It must match these, or you'll hear nothing:

Frequency910.525 MHz
Bandwidth62.5 kHz
SpreadingSF7
Coding rateCR5

New radios often ship on the European 869 MHz setting. Always check this step.

Then set Path hash size to 2 bytes. In the app it lives under the Optimise screen on recent versions, or Experimental settings on older ones. It needs firmware 1.14 or newer on your radio; Indiana's repeaters already run 2-byte or newer, so this is just about getting your own device caught up.

Why these numbers: supported frequencies in the MeshCore FAQ; path hash size is explained in the MeshCore 1.14 release notes.

Say hello

  • Send a flood advert (the app's "announce me to everyone" button) so nearby people and repeaters learn about you.
  • Post in Public, or join #test and send a test message.
  • Seeing "heard repeats" after you send means a repeater picked you up.

What to expect: your radio only has to reach one repeater, not the whole state; the repeaters do the rest. If you hear nothing, the fix is almost always height and a nearby repeater, not a different radio. Recheck step 4, put the radio in a window or upstairs, then ask in the Indiana Mesh Discord where the nearest repeater is.

Words you'll see, in plain English
  • CompanionA radio you carry and control from your phone over Bluetooth.
  • RepeaterA fixed radio, usually on a roof or tower, that relays everyone's messages. Volunteers run them.
  • Room serverA fixed radio that also stores recent messages so you catch up when you come back into range.
  • ObserverA radio connected to the internet that uploads what it hears, so the live maps show real activity.
  • Flood advertA hello sent to everyone in range, so people and repeaters add you to their lists.
  • ChannelA group chat. Public is built in; hashtag channels like #test are open to anyone who adds the name.
  • PresetThe radio settings (frequency, bandwidth, SF, CR) everyone in a region shares. Radios on different presets can't hear each other.
  • Region / scopeOptional tags repeater owners set so some traffic stays local. Rarely used; ask your local group whether they use them before setting one.
  • Path hash sizeHow many bytes identify each hop in a message's path. 2 bytes names hops more accurately; Indiana's repeaters already run 2-byte or newer, it just needs firmware 1.14 or newer on your own radio.
  • Heard repeatsThe app's sign that a repeater picked up your message.

Hear everything around you

Regions: rare, so ask

Most people leave region and default scope unset; unscoped messages go out to everyone, and you still receive everything that reaches you. If the people near you do use regions on their repeaters or companions, match them.

Join the channels people use

Public is there from the start. Add hashtag channels like #test, #hamradio or #wardriving. In #porchlight, people share the names of channels worth joining.

Get some height

A window or a higher shelf helps more than anything else. Your radio only needs to reach one repeater; the repeaters do the rest.

Near a state line?

Nothing extra to set. Ohio, Kentucky, Michigan and Illinois use the same radio settings as Indiana, so you hear repeaters on both sides.

  • Unscoped messages are what people across the line hear, and what their replies ride back on.
  • Join the group on the other side too: their channels and people are part of your local mesh.
  • A scoped message stops at the first repeater without its tag. Unscoped is what crosses borders.

Running a repeater?

Companion users need nothing beyond the steps above. Repeater owners: the official MeshCore docs cover setup and the CLI. Neighbouring groups publish their own region names; follow theirs exactly where your repeater can hear them.
